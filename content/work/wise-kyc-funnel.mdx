## Why this matters

Wise only makes money when users finish onboarding and send their first transfer. Unlike SaaS, where you can win users back with emails or offers, a failed KYC usually means the user switches to Revolut or Monzo and never returns. For a neobank, onboarding completion isn't a growth metric. It's existential.

## How I approached it

- **Lived the product:** signed up for Wise and documented every screen of a 22-step onboarding flow
- **Listened to users:** read public feedback on Trustpilot, Reddit and app store reviews
- **Benchmarked:** compared Wise's onboarding with Revolut and Monzo
- **Modelled the funnel:** built a simulated 10,000-user dataset in Python based on published fintech onboarding benchmarks
- **Queried it:** ran 5 SQL queries to find drop-off patterns, then visualised them in Looker Studio

<Callout title="A note on the data">
Real Wise data isn't public, so the funnel uses a simulated dataset built from industry benchmarks (typical KYC completion of 60–75% for digital banks). The product observations and user research are real.
</Callout>

**KYC ID submission is the single biggest leak in the flow (32.9%).** For every 10,000 sign-ups, 2,194 users are lost at this one step.

### Why users drop
Going through the flow myself, the document requirement appeared mid-journey, after I'd already set an amount and added a recipient, with no warning about which documents were needed or how long verification takes. Public feedback told the same story: unexpected document requirements, photos rejected for glare or cropped corners, and no explanation when verification fails. Reddit threads described rejected employment contracts with no guidance on alternatives, and repeated re-verification ending in account closure.

**Users aren't unwilling to verify. They're blindsided by the process.**

## Competitive teardown

| | Wise | Revolut | Monzo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding | ~14 screens | ~5 steps, app-only | ~5 steps, app-first |
| KYC timing | Mid-transfer flow | During sign-up | During sign-up |
| India availability | Full access | Controlled beta | Not available |
| Biggest friction | No advance warning of document needs | Opaque when verification stalls | Manual review delays |
| Does well | Fee transparency, UPI auto-detection | Speed on simple cases | Layered automated verification |

Wise is the only one of the three that springs KYC on users mid-journey. Revolut and Monzo verify upfront, so users know what's coming before they invest time. And because Revolut is still in beta in India, Indian users, who face the most document-heavy path on Wise, don't yet have a direct alternative. That window won't stay open.

## The experiment

**Hypothesis:** if we show a pre-KYC explainer (accepted documents, expected time, what happens next), KYC submission will rise from 67.1% to 74%, because users abandon out of uncertainty, not unwillingness.

| Control (A) | Variant (B) |
| --- | --- |
| Personal details → KYC upload, no context | Personal details → explainer screen (accepted documents, 2–5 min estimate, next steps, "60% complete" progress) → KYC upload |

**Metrics**
- **Primary:** KYC ID submission rate, 67.1% → 74%
- **Secondary:** overall activation, 18.4% → ~21%
- **Guardrail:** time to KYC submission, so motivated users aren't slowed down

**Sample size:** a two-proportion z-test (α = 0.05, 80% power) needs **686 users per variant, 1,372 in total**, about 1–2 weeks of traffic.

**Decision rules**
- B wins at 95% significance → ship to 100% and watch the guardrail for 2 weeks
- B loses → interview users who dropped at KYC to find the real cause
- Inconclusive → extend one week, then reassess

## Next steps

1. Run the pre-KYC explainer experiment.
2. Segment KYC drop-off by country. Indian users face document uploads UK and US users don't, which points to a targeted fix for high-friction markets.
3. Tackle the second-biggest leak, the liveness check (29.4%), with a separate experiment on real-time camera guidance.
